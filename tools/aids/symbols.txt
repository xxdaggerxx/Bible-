# Symbols: marked only in the verses where the Bible uses them as symbols.
# Meanings: where the Bible explains a symbol itself, that first; otherwise the traditional reading.

== The Lamb
kind: symbol
forms: lamb, lambs, lamb's
refs: Gen 22:7-8; Exod 12:3-5,21; Isa 53:7; Jer 11:19; John 1:29,36; Acts 8:32; 1Cor 5:7; 1Pet 1:19; Rev 5:6,8,12-13; Rev 6:1,16; Rev 7:9-10,14,17; Rev 12:11; Rev 13:8; Rev 14:1,4,10; Rev 15:3; Rev 17:14; Rev 19:7,9; Rev 21:9,14,22-23,27; Rev 22:1,3
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Jesus, the sacrifice for sin. John the Baptist called him "the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29), fulfilling the Passover lamb and Isaiah's servant led "as a lamb to the slaughter". In Revelation the slain Lamb stands on the throne, worshipped by all heaven, and conquers.

== The lion
kind: symbol
forms: lion, lions, lion's, lioness
refs: Gen 49:9; Num 24:9; Prov 28:1; Ezek 1:10; Dan 7:4; Hos 5:14; Amos 3:8; 1Pet 5:8; Rev 4:7; Rev 5:5; Rev 13:2
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Strength, courage and royal power. Jacob called Judah a lion, and Christ is "the Lion of the tribe of Judah" (Rev 5:5); the Lord's voice roars like a lion (Amos 3:8). But the devil too "walketh about, as a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour" (1 Pet 5:8). In Daniel 7 a lion with wings stands for Babylon.

== The dove
kind: symbol
forms: dove, doves, dove's, turtledove, turtle
refs: Gen 8:8-12; Ps 55:6; Song 2:14; Song 5:2; Hos 7:11; Matt 3:16; Matt 10:16; Mark 1:10; Luke 3:22; John 1:32
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The Holy Spirit, who came down on Jesus at his baptism "like a dove". The dove also stands for gentleness and innocence ("harmless as doves", Matt 10:16), for peace (the dove with the olive leaf after the flood), and in the Song of Songs for the beloved.

== The serpent and the dragon
kind: symbol
forms: serpent, serpents, dragon, dragons, snake, leviathan, old serpent, ancient serpent
refs: Gen 3:1-15; Job 41:1; Ps 74:13-14; Isa 27:1; 2Cor 11:3; Rev 12:3-17; Rev 13:2,4,11; Rev 16:13; Rev 20:2
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Satan, the deceiver. The serpent tempted Eve in Eden; Revelation names "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world" (Rev 12:9) and shows the great red dragon defeated and bound. God's promise that the woman's offspring would crush the serpent's head (Gen 3:15) is read as the first promise of Christ.

== The bronze serpent
kind: symbol
forms: serpent, serpents, snake, bronze, brass, brasen, nehushtan
refs: Num 21:6-9; 2Kgs 18:4; John 3:14-15
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
When Israel was dying from snake bites, God told Moses to set a bronze snake on a pole: whoever looked at it lived. Jesus said, "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up" on the cross, so that whoever believes may have eternal life.

== The beasts
kind: symbol
forms: beast, beasts, beast's
refs: Dan 7:3-23; Dan 8:4; Rev 11:7; Rev 13:1-18; Rev 14:9,11; Rev 15:2; Rev 16:2,10,13; Rev 17:3-17; Rev 19:19-20; Rev 20:4,10
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Worldly kingdoms and powers that set themselves against God and his people. Daniel is told that his four beasts "are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth" (Dan 7:17). In Revelation the beast from the sea makes war on the saints and demands worship, but is thrown into the lake of fire.

== The living creatures
kind: symbol
forms: living creatures, living creature, beasts, creatures, beast, cherubim, cherubims, cherub, faces
refs: Ezek 1:5-25; Ezek 10:1-22; Rev 4:6-9; Rev 5:6-14; Rev 6:1-7; Rev 7:11; Rev 14:3; Rev 15:7; Rev 19:4
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Heavenly beings around God's throne, full of eyes, with the faces of a lion, an ox, a man and an eagle, crying "Holy, holy, holy". They show God's majesty and the worship of all creation. Since early times Christians have also linked the four faces with the four Gospels.

== Horns
kind: symbol
forms: horn, horns
refs: 1Sam 2:1,10; Ps 18:2; Ps 75:4-5,10; Ps 89:17,24; Ps 92:10; Ps 112:9; Ps 132:17; Dan 7:7-8,11,20-24; Dan 8:3-22; Zech 1:18-21; Luke 1:69; Rev 5:6; Rev 12:3; Rev 13:1,11; Rev 17:3,7,12,16
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Power and strength, like the horns of a strong animal; "the horn of salvation" is the strong Saviour God raised up (Luke 1:69). In Daniel and Revelation horns are kings: "the ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings" (Dan 7:24). The Lamb's seven horns show his perfect power.

== The eagle
kind: symbol
forms: eagle, eagles, eagle's, eagles'
refs: Exod 19:4; Deut 32:11; Ps 103:5; Isa 40:31; Ezek 1:10; Ezek 17:3,7; Dan 7:4; Rev 4:7; Rev 12:14
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's care and strength: he carried Israel "on eagles' wings" out of Egypt, and those who wait on him "shall mount up with wings as eagles" (Isa 40:31). In Ezekiel 17 the eagles are great kings.

== Sheep
kind: symbol
forms: sheep, flock, flocks, lambs, ewe
refs: Ps 23; Ps 74:1; Ps 79:13; Ps 95:7; Ps 100:3; Isa 40:11; Isa 53:6; Jer 23:1-4; Jer 50:6; Ezek 34; Zech 13:7; Matt 9:36; Matt 10:6,16; Matt 15:24; Matt 18:12-13; Matt 25:32-33; Matt 26:31; Luke 15:4-7; John 10:1-30; John 21:15-17; Heb 13:20; 1Pet 2:25; 1Pet 5:2-3
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's people: needing a shepherd, prone to wander, cared for and led. "All we like sheep have gone astray" (Isa 53:6); "we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture" (Ps 100:3). At the judgment Christ separates the sheep (the righteous) from the goats.

== The shepherd
kind: symbol
forms: shepherd, shepherds, shepherd's, pastor, pastors
refs: Gen 49:24; Ps 23:1; Ps 80:1; Isa 40:11; Jer 23:1-4; Ezek 34; Zech 11:4-17; Zech 13:7; Matt 26:31; Mark 14:27; John 10:11-16; Heb 13:20; 1Pet 2:25; 1Pet 5:2-4; Rev 7:17
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
One who leads, feeds and protects God's people. "The Lord is my shepherd" (Ps 23:1). Kings and leaders were called shepherds, and the prophets condemned the bad ones. Jesus is "the good shepherd" who gives his life for the sheep (John 10:11), and church leaders are shepherds under him.

== Wolves
kind: symbol
forms: wolf, wolves
refs: Isa 11:6; Isa 65:25; Ezek 22:27; Zeph 3:3; Matt 7:15; Matt 10:16; Luke 10:3; John 10:12; Acts 20:29
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
False teachers and the enemies of God's people, who come to tear the flock: "false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves" (Matt 7:15). In Isaiah the wolf living with the lamb pictures the peace of God's coming kingdom.

== Fishers of men
kind: symbol
forms: fishers, fishers of men, fish, fishes, net, nets, catch men
refs: Jer 16:16; Matt 4:19; Mark 1:17; Luke 5:10; Matt 13:47-50; John 21:6-11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Jesus called fishermen to become "fishers of men" (Matt 4:19), gathering people into God's kingdom. In the parable of the net, the catch of good and bad fish pictures the final judgment.

== Locusts
kind: symbol
forms: locust, locusts, cankerworm, palmerworm, caterpillar, grasshoppers
refs: Joel 1:4; Joel 2:1-11,25; Amos 7:1; Nah 3:15-17; Rev 9:3-11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Swarms of locusts that strip the land bare stand for God's judgment and invading armies; Joel describes an army like locusts. God promises, "I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten" (Joel 2:25).

== The horses and their riders
kind: symbol
forms: horse, horses, horseman, horsemen, rider, riders, red horse, white horse, black horse, pale horse
refs: Zech 1:8-11; Zech 6:1-8; Rev 6:2-8; Rev 9:16-19; Rev 19:11-21
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
In Zechariah horses of different colours are God's messengers patrolling the earth. In Revelation 6 four riders bring conquest (white), war (red), famine (black) and death (pale). In Revelation 19 Christ himself rides a white horse, "Faithful and True", to judge and make war in righteousness.

== The vine
kind: symbol
forms: vine, vines, vineyard, vineyards, branch, branches, husbandman, vinedresser
refs: Ps 80:8-16; Isa 5:1-7; Isa 27:2-6; Jer 2:21; Ezek 15; Ezek 19:10-14; Hos 10:1; Matt 21:28-43; Mark 12:1-12; Luke 13:6-9; Luke 20:9-16; John 15:1-8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's people. Israel was the vine God planted and cared for, which should have borne good fruit: "the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel" (Isa 5:7). Jesus said, "I am the true vine, ye are the branches" (John 15:5): life and fruit come only from staying joined to him.

== The Branch
kind: symbol
forms: branch, shoot, rod, stem, sprout, root, offshoot, twig
refs: Isa 4:2; Isa 11:1,10; Isa 53:2; Jer 23:5; Jer 33:15; Ezek 17:22-23; Zech 3:8; Zech 6:12; Rom 15:12; Rev 5:5; Rev 22:16
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A title of the Messiah: a new shoot growing from the cut-down stump of Jesse, David's father. "I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign" (Jer 23:5). The New Testament sees it fulfilled in Jesus, "the root and the offspring of David" (Rev 22:16).

== The fig tree
kind: symbol
forms: fig tree, fig trees, fig, figs
refs: Jer 8:13; Jer 24:1-10; Hos 9:10; Joel 1:7; Mic 4:4; Matt 21:19-21; Matt 24:32-33; Mark 11:13-14,20-21; Mark 13:28-29; Luke 13:6-9; Luke 21:29-31
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Israel, expected to bear fruit for God. Jesus cursed a fig tree full of leaves but with no fruit, a picture of religion with no true faith, and told the parable of the barren fig tree given one more year. "Every man under his fig tree" pictures peace and plenty (Mic 4:4).

== The olive tree
kind: symbol
forms: olive tree, olive trees, olive, olives, wild olive
refs: Ps 52:8; Ps 128:3; Jer 11:16; Hos 14:6; Zech 4:3,11-14; Rom 11:16-24; Rev 11:4
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's people, rich and fruitful: "I am like a green olive tree in the house of God" (Ps 52:8). Paul pictures Israel as a cultivated olive tree into which Gentile believers, wild branches, have been grafted. In Zechariah and Revelation two olive trees supply oil: God's anointed witnesses.

== The cedar
kind: symbol
forms: cedar, cedars, cedar of lebanon, cedars of lebanon
refs: Ps 92:12; Isa 2:13; Ezek 17:3,22-23; Ezek 31:3-18; Amos 2:9
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The tall, strong cedars of Lebanon stand for greatness and lasting strength: "the righteous shall grow like a cedar in Lebanon" (Ps 92:12). Proud nations are pictured as cedars God brings down.

== The mustard seed
kind: symbol
forms: mustard seed, grain of mustard seed, mustard, seed
refs: Matt 13:31-32; Matt 17:20; Mark 4:30-32; Luke 13:18-19; Luke 17:6
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The tiniest of seeds that grows into a great plant: God's kingdom, small in its beginning, grows to fill the world. Faith as small as a mustard seed, trusting a great God, can move mountains.

== Seed
kind: symbol
forms: seed, seeds, sower, soweth, sow, sowed, sown
refs: Gen 3:15; Gen 22:18; Mark 4:3-20; Matt 13:3-23; Luke 8:5-15; John 12:24; 1Cor 15:36-44; Gal 3:16; 1Pet 1:23
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
In the parable of the sower, "the seed is the word of God" (Luke 8:11), received differently by different hearts. A grain of wheat dying to bear much fruit pictures Jesus' death and our resurrection. The promised "seed" of the woman and of Abraham is Christ (Gal 3:16).

== Wheat and chaff
kind: symbol
forms: wheat, chaff, tares, weeds, darnel, stubble
refs: Ps 1:4; Ps 35:5; Isa 17:13; Hos 13:3; Mal 4:1; Matt 3:12; Matt 13:24-30,36-43; Luke 3:17; Luke 22:31
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The righteous and the wicked, growing together until the harvest, when they are separated. Chaff, light and worthless, blows away: "the ungodly are not so: but are like the chaff which the wind driveth away" (Ps 1:4).

== Thorns
kind: symbol
forms: thorns, thorn, thistles, briers, brambles, thornbushes
refs: Gen 3:18; Isa 5:6; Isa 55:13; Matt 7:16; Matt 13:7,22; Matt 27:29; Mark 15:17; John 19:2,5; 2Cor 12:7; Heb 6:8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The curse of sin on the ground (Gen 3:18) and the worries and riches that choke God's word (Matt 13:22). Jesus wore a crown of thorns, bearing the curse; Paul's "thorn in the flesh" was a painful trial that taught him God's grace is enough.

== Grass and flowers
kind: symbol
forms: grass, flower, flowers, flower of the field
refs: Ps 37:2; Ps 90:5-6; Ps 103:15-16; Isa 40:6-8; Matt 6:28-30; Jas 1:10-11; 1Pet 1:24-25
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
How short human life is: "All flesh is grass... The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever" (Isa 40:6-8).

== The tree of life
kind: symbol
forms: tree of life, tree
refs: Gen 2:9; Gen 3:22,24; Prov 3:18; Prov 11:30; Prov 13:12; Prov 15:4; Rev 2:7; Rev 22:2,14,19
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The tree in Eden whose fruit gave everlasting life, closed to Adam and Eve after the fall. It appears again in the new Jerusalem, open to all who are washed and made clean, "for the healing of the nations" (Rev 22:2): eternal life restored.

== A tree by the water
kind: symbol
forms: tree, trees, planted
refs: Ps 1:3; Jer 17:7-8; Ezek 47:12
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The person who delights in God's word and trusts in him is "like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season" (Ps 1:3): steady, fruitful and green even in drought.

== Hyssop
kind: symbol
forms: hyssop
refs: Exod 12:22; Lev 14:4-6,49-52; Num 19:6,18; Ps 51:7; John 19:29; Heb 9:19
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A small bushy plant used to sprinkle blood or water in cleansing rites, as on the doorposts at Passover. David prayed, "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean" (Ps 51:7). Jesus was offered sour wine on a hyssop branch at the cross.

== The rod and the sceptre
kind: symbol
forms: rod, staff, sceptre, scepter, rod of iron, iron rod
refs: Gen 49:10; Num 17:1-10; Num 24:17; Ps 2:9; Ps 23:4; Ps 45:6; Ps 110:2; Isa 11:4; Heb 1:8; Heb 9:4; Rev 2:27; Rev 12:5; Rev 19:15
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Authority and rule. "The sceptre shall not depart from Judah" (Gen 49:10) promises a lasting king; the Messiah rules the nations "with a rod of iron" (Ps 2:9). Aaron's rod that budded showed God's chosen priest. The shepherd's rod and staff in Psalm 23 mean protection and guidance.

== Bread
kind: symbol
forms: bread, loaf, loaves, bread of life, living bread
refs: Deut 8:3; Matt 4:4; Matt 26:26; Mark 14:22; Luke 22:19; Luke 24:30-35; John 6:32-58; Acts 2:42; 1Cor 10:16-17; 1Cor 11:23-27
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
What gives and sustains life. "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Matt 4:4). Jesus said, "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35); at the Last Supper the broken bread stood for his body given for us.

== Manna
kind: symbol
forms: manna, bread from heaven, bread of heaven, hidden manna
refs: Exod 16:4,14-35; Num 11:6-9; Deut 8:3,16; Neh 9:15,20; Ps 78:24-25; John 6:31-35,48-51,58; Heb 9:4; Rev 2:17
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The bread God sent from heaven each morning to feed Israel in the wilderness for forty years. Jesus said he is the true bread from heaven: "your fathers did eat manna in the wilderness, and are dead... if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever" (John 6:49-51).

== The cup
kind: symbol
forms: cup, cups
refs: Ps 11:6; Ps 16:5; Ps 23:5; Ps 75:8; Ps 116:13; Isa 51:17,22; Jer 25:15-28; Matt 20:22-23; Matt 26:27-29,39,42; Mark 10:38-39; Mark 14:23-24,36; Luke 22:17-20,42; John 18:11; 1Cor 10:16,21; 1Cor 11:25-27; Rev 14:10; Rev 16:19; Rev 17:4; Rev 18:6
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A person's portion or lot from God: a cup of blessing and salvation, or the cup of God's wrath against sin. In Gethsemane Jesus prayed that "this cup" might pass from him, yet drank it for us. At the Last Supper the cup of wine is "the new testament in my blood".

== Wine
kind: symbol
forms: wine, new wine
refs: Ps 104:15; Prov 9:2,5; Isa 25:6; Isa 55:1; Amos 9:13; Joel 3:18; Matt 9:17; John 2:1-11; Rev 14:8,10; Rev 16:19; Rev 17:2; Rev 18:3
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Joy and blessing: the coming kingdom is a feast "of wines on the lees" (Isa 25:6), and Jesus' first sign turned water into the best wine. New wine pictures the new life he brings. In Revelation Babylon makes the nations drunk with the wine of her immorality, and the wicked drink the wine of God's wrath.

== Water
kind: symbol
forms: water, waters, living water, fountain, fountains, river, rivers, springs, wells
refs: Ps 1:3; Ps 23:2; Ps 42:1; Ps 46:4; Isa 12:3; Isa 44:3; Isa 55:1; Jer 2:13; Jer 17:13; Ezek 36:25; Ezek 47:1-12; Zech 14:8; John 3:5; John 4:10-15; John 7:37-39; Eph 5:26; Titus 3:5; Rev 7:17; Rev 21:6; Rev 22:1,17
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Life, cleansing and the Holy Spirit. God is "the fountain of living waters" (Jer 2:13); Jesus offers living water that becomes "a well of water springing up into everlasting life" (John 4:14), which John explains is the Spirit (John 7:39). The river of life flows from God's throne in the new creation.

== Light
kind: symbol
forms: light, lights, lamp, shine, shineth, shined
refs: Gen 1:3; Ps 27:1; Ps 36:9; Ps 119:105,130; Isa 9:2; Isa 42:6; Isa 49:6; Isa 60:1-3,19-20; Matt 4:16; Matt 5:14-16; Luke 2:32; John 1:4-9; John 3:19-21; John 8:12; John 9:5; John 12:35-36,46; Acts 26:18; 2Cor 4:6; Eph 5:8-14; Col 1:12; 1Pet 2:9; 1John 1:5-7; 1John 2:8-10; Rev 21:23-24; Rev 22:5
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's presence, truth, holiness and life. "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5). Jesus is "the light of the world" (John 8:12), and his people are to shine as lights. God's word is "a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path".

== Darkness
kind: symbol
forms: darkness, dark, night, shadow of death, outer darkness
refs: Isa 9:2; Isa 60:2; Matt 4:16; Matt 6:23; Matt 8:12; Matt 22:13; Matt 25:30; John 1:5; John 3:19; John 8:12; John 12:35,46; Acts 26:18; Rom 13:12; Eph 5:8,11; Eph 6:12; Col 1:13; 1Thess 5:4-5; 1Pet 2:9; 1John 1:5-6; 1John 2:8-11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Sin, ignorance and evil, and the realm of Satan from which God rescues his people: "who hath delivered us from the power of darkness" (Col 1:13). "Outer darkness" is the final separation from God.

== The lamp
kind: symbol
forms: lamp, lamps, candle, candles
refs: 2Sam 22:29; Ps 18:28; Ps 119:105; Prov 6:23; Prov 13:9; Prov 20:27; Matt 5:15; Matt 6:22; Matt 25:1-10; Mark 4:21; Luke 8:16; Luke 11:33-36; Luke 12:35; Rev 21:23
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's word giving guidance ("a lamp unto my feet", Ps 119:105), the life of the righteous, and the witness of believers, not to be hidden under a basket. The wise virgins' lamps with oil picture being ready for Christ's coming.

== The lampstands
kind: symbol
forms: lampstand, lampstands, candlestick, candlesticks
refs: Zech 4:2,11; Rev 1:12-13,20; Rev 2:1,5; Rev 11:4
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
In Revelation the Bible explains it: "the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches" (Rev 1:20). The church holds out the light of Christ, who walks among the lampstands; a church that loses its first love may have its lampstand removed.

== Salt
kind: symbol
forms: salt, salted, salty, savour, saltiness
refs: Lev 2:13; Num 18:19; 2Chr 13:5; Matt 5:13; Mark 9:49-50; Luke 14:34-35; Col 4:6
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Salt preserves and flavours. Jesus called his disciples "the salt of the earth" (Matt 5:13), a good influence in a decaying world. A "covenant of salt" is a lasting, unbreakable covenant; Christians' speech should be "seasoned with salt" (Col 4:6).

== Leaven (yeast)
kind: symbol
forms: leaven, leavened, leaveneth, yeast
refs: Exod 12:15-20; Matt 13:33; Matt 16:6-12; Mark 8:15; Luke 12:1; Luke 13:20-21; 1Cor 5:6-8; Gal 5:9
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A little leaven spreads through the whole lump of dough. Most often it pictures sin and false teaching that spread: "beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy" (Luke 12:1). In one parable it shows the hidden growth of God's kingdom (Matt 13:33).

== Oil
kind: symbol
forms: oil, ointment, oil of gladness, oil of joy, anointing
refs: Ps 23:5; Ps 45:7; Ps 133:2; Isa 61:1-3; Zech 4:11-14; Matt 25:3-8; Heb 1:9; Jas 5:14; 1John 2:20,27
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The Holy Spirit and God's blessing and joy. Kings and priests were anointed with oil when God's Spirit came on them for their work; Christ was anointed "with the oil of gladness" (Heb 1:9). Believers too have "an unction from the Holy One" (1 John 2:20).

== Blood
kind: symbol
forms: blood
refs: Gen 4:10; Exod 12:7,13,22-23; Exod 24:6-8; Lev 17:11; Matt 26:28; Mark 14:24; Luke 22:20; John 6:53-56; Acts 20:28; Rom 3:25; Rom 5:9; Eph 1:7; Eph 2:13; Col 1:20; Heb 9:12-22; Heb 10:19; Heb 12:24; Heb 13:12,20; 1Pet 1:2,19; 1John 1:7; Rev 1:5; Rev 5:9; Rev 7:14; Rev 12:11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Life given in death. "The life of the flesh is in the blood... it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Lev 17:11). The blood of the Passover lamb saved Israel; the blood of Christ, shed on the cross, cleanses from all sin and seals the new covenant.

== Fire
kind: symbol
forms: fire, flame, flames, fiery, flaming, burning
refs: Exod 3:2; Exod 13:21; Exod 19:18; Deut 4:24; Isa 6:6-7; Jer 23:29; Mal 3:2-3; Matt 3:11-12; Luke 3:16; Acts 2:3; 1Cor 3:13-15; Heb 12:29; 1Pet 1:7; Rev 1:14; Rev 19:12; Rev 20:10,14-15; Matt 25:41; Mark 9:43-48
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's holy presence ("our God is a consuming fire", Heb 12:29), his purifying work, like a refiner's fire, and his judgment on sin. The Holy Spirit came at Pentecost as tongues of fire.

== The cloud
kind: symbol
forms: cloud, clouds, pillar of cloud, cloudy pillar
refs: Exod 13:21-22; Exod 14:19-24; Exod 16:10; Exod 19:9,16; Exod 24:15-18; Exod 33:9-10; Exod 40:34-38; 1Kgs 8:10-11; Dan 7:13; Matt 17:5; Matt 24:30; Matt 26:64; Mark 9:7; Luke 9:34-35; Acts 1:9-11; 1Thess 4:17; Rev 1:7; Rev 14:14
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's glorious presence, both shown and hidden. A pillar of cloud led Israel and filled the tabernacle and temple; a cloud covered Jesus at the transfiguration and received him at his ascension. The Son of Man comes "with the clouds of heaven" (Dan 7:13; Rev 1:7).

== Wind and breath
kind: symbol
forms: wind, winds, breath, breathe, breathed, rushing mighty wind
refs: Gen 2:7; Ezek 37:5-14; John 3:8; John 20:22; Acts 2:2
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The Holy Spirit: in Hebrew and Greek the same word means wind, breath and spirit. God breathed life into Adam; the wind of the Spirit gave life to the dry bones in Ezekiel; Jesus compared new birth by the Spirit to the wind (John 3:8) and breathed on his disciples, saying "Receive ye the Holy Ghost".

== The rock
kind: symbol
forms: rock, rocks, stone
refs: Exod 17:6; Num 20:8-11; Deut 32:4,15,18,30-31; 1Sam 2:2; 2Sam 22:2-3,32,47; Ps 18:2,31,46; Ps 19:14; Ps 31:2-3; Ps 61:2; Ps 62:2,6-7; Ps 95:1; Isa 26:4; Isa 32:2; Matt 7:24-25; Matt 16:18; Rom 9:33; 1Cor 10:4; 1Pet 2:8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God himself, firm, safe and unchanging: "The Lord is my rock, and my fortress" (Ps 18:2). The rock that gave water in the wilderness "was Christ" (1 Cor 10:4). A life built on Jesus' words is a house built on rock.

== The cornerstone
kind: symbol
forms: cornerstone, corner stone, chief corner stone, head of the corner, capstone, chief cornerstone, stone, cornerstone of the foundation

refs: Deut 8:2-3; Hos 2:14; Isa 35:1-6; Isa 40:3; Matt 4:1; Mark 1:12-13; 1Cor 10:1-11; Heb 3:7-19; Rev 12:6,14
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A place of testing, humbling and dependence on God, where Israel learned that man lives by every word of God, and where Jesus was tempted forty days. It is also where God meets and protects his people, and where the way of the Lord is prepared.

== Rest
kind: symbol
forms: rest, sabbath rest, sabbath-rest, my rest, his rest, enter into rest
refs: Ps 95:11; Isa 11:10; Isa 30:15; Matt 11:28-29; Heb 3:11,18; Heb 4:1-11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The promised land was Israel's rest, but Hebrews says a greater rest remains for God's people: ceasing from our own works and trusting Christ, and the eternal rest to come. "Come unto me... and I will give you rest" (Matt 11:28).

== Fruit
kind: symbol
forms: fruit, fruits, fruitful, bear fruit, bring forth fruit
refs: Ps 1:3; Matt 3:8,10; Matt 7:16-20; Matt 12:33; John 15:2-16; Rom 7:4; Gal 5:22-23; Phil 1:11; Col 1:10; Heb 12:11; Jas 3:17-18
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The visible results of a life, good or bad: "by their fruits ye shall know them" (Matt 7:20). "The fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance" (Gal 5:22-23), growing in those joined to Christ.

== The pearl and the treasure
kind: symbol
forms: pearl, pearls, treasure, treasures, pearl of great price, hid treasure
refs: Matt 6:19-21; Matt 7:6; Matt 13:44-46; 2Cor 4:7; Col 2:3
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The kingdom of heaven, worth more than everything else: a man sells all he has to buy a field with hidden treasure, a merchant to buy one pearl of great price. Jesus told us to lay up treasure in heaven.

== The body of Christ
kind: symbol
forms: body, bodies, members, member, one body
refs: Rom 12:4-5; 1Cor 10:16-17; 1Cor 12:12-27; Eph 1:22-23; Eph 4:4,12,16; Eph 5:23,29-30; Col 1:18,24; Col 2:19; Col 3:15
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The church: "now ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular" (1 Cor 12:27). Like the parts of a body, believers are joined to one another and to Christ the head, each with a part to play.

== Christ the head
kind: symbol
forms: head, headship
refs: 1Cor 11:3; Eph 1:22; Eph 4:15; Eph 5:23; Col 1:18; Col 2:10,19
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Christ is "the head of the body, the church" (Col 1:18): its source of life, its ruler and the one it grows up into.

== Foundations and building
kind: symbol
forms: foundation, foundations, building, built, builder, edifice, house, master builder
refs: Matt 7:24-27; 1Cor 3:9-15; Eph 2:19-22; 1Tim 3:15; 2Tim 2:19; Heb 3:3-6; Heb 11:10; 1Pet 2:5; Rev 21:14,19
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's people are a building he is making, "built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone" (Eph 2:20). "Other foundation can no man lay than that is laid, which is Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 3:11).

== The race
kind: symbol
forms: race, run, runneth, ran, running, prize, course, finish the race, finished my course, crown
refs: 1Cor 9:24-27; Gal 2:2; Gal 5:7; Phil 2:16; Phil 3:12-14; 2Tim 2:5; 2Tim 4:7; Heb 12:1-2
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The Christian life is a race to be run with endurance, looking to Jesus, the goal and the prize. Paul at the end could say, "I have finished my course, I have kept the faith" (2 Tim 4:7).

== The watchman
kind: symbol
forms: watchman, watchmen, watch
refs: Isa 21:6-12; Isa 52:8; Isa 62:6; Jer 6:17; Ezek 3:17; Ezek 33:2-9; Hab 2:1; Heb 13:17
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The prophet as a lookout on the city wall, warning of danger: "I have made thee a watchman unto the house of Israel" (Ezek 3:17). If he fails to warn, he is responsible; church leaders too "watch for your souls" (Heb 13:17).

== Wings
kind: symbol
forms: wings, wing, under his wings, shadow of thy wings
refs: Ruth 2:12; Ps 17:8; Ps 36:7; Ps 57:1; Ps 61:4; Ps 63:7; Ps 91:4; Mal 4:2; Matt 23:37; Luke 13:34
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's tender protection, like a bird sheltering its young: "he shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his wings shalt thou trust" (Ps 91:4). Jesus longed to gather Jerusalem "as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings".

== Shield, fortress and tower
kind: symbol
forms: shield, fortress, refuge, tower, strong tower, buckler, stronghold, high tower, strength
refs: Gen 15:1; Ps 3:3; Ps 18:2; Ps 28:7; Ps 46:1,7,11; Ps 61:3; Ps 84:11; Ps 91:2; Ps 144:2; Prov 18:10
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
God's protection of his people: "The name of the Lord is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe" (Prov 18:10); "God is our refuge and strength" (Ps 46:1).

== The anchor
kind: symbol
forms: anchor
refs: Heb 6:19
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Christian hope in God's promise is "an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast" (Heb 6:19), holding firm in storms because it is fixed in Christ, who has gone ahead into God's presence.

== The mirror
kind: symbol
forms: glass, mirror, through a glass, darkly
refs: 1Cor 13:12; 2Cor 3:18; Jas 1:23-25
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Ancient mirrors of polished metal gave only a dim image: "now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face" (1 Cor 13:12). James likens God's word to a mirror that shows us as we are.

== The mark and the name on the forehead
kind: symbol
forms: mark, forehead, foreheads, seal of god, his name, the mark of the beast
refs: Ezek 9:4; Rev 7:3; Rev 9:4; Rev 13:16-17; Rev 14:1,9,11; Rev 16:2; Rev 19:20; Rev 20:4; Rev 22:4
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Showing who a person belongs to. God's servants are sealed with his name on their foreheads; those who follow the beast take its mark. Traditional interpreters read it as the outward sign of a person's loyalty, whether to God or to the world's power.

== The bottomless pit
kind: symbol
forms: bottomless pit, abyss, pit, deep
refs: Luke 8:31; Rom 10:7; Rev 9:1-2,11; Rev 11:7; Rev 17:8; Rev 20:1,3
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The abyss: the prison of evil spirits, from which the demons begged Jesus not to send them, and where Satan is bound in Revelation 20.

== The four winds and corners
kind: symbol
forms: four winds, four corners, corners of the earth, four quarters, winds
refs: Isa 11:12; Jer 49:36; Ezek 37:9; Dan 7:2; Dan 8:8; Zech 2:6; Zech 6:5; Matt 24:31; Mark 13:27; Rev 7:1; Rev 20:8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
North, south, east and west: the whole earth. The elect are gathered "from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other" (Matt 24:31).

== The two witnesses
kind: symbol
forms: two witnesses, witnesses, two olive trees, two candlesticks
refs: Rev 11:3-12
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Two prophets in Revelation 11 who testify for God, are killed by the beast, and are raised and taken up to heaven. They are described like Moses and Elijah; many traditional interpreters see in them the faithful witness of God's people, which cannot finally be silenced.

== The woman clothed with the sun
kind: symbol
forms: woman, a woman clothed with the sun
refs: Rev 12:1-17
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A woman crowned with twelve stars gives birth to the male child who will rule the nations, Christ, while the dragon tries to destroy him. Traditional Protestant interpreters see her as God's people, Israel and the church, from whom the Messiah came and whom God protects.

== Colours: white
kind: symbol
forms: white, whiter
refs: Isa 1:18; Dan 7:9; Matt 17:2; Matt 28:3; Mark 9:3; Rev 1:14; Rev 2:17; Rev 3:4-5,18; Rev 4:4; Rev 6:2,11; Rev 7:9,13-14; Rev 19:11,14; Rev 20:11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Purity, holiness and victory: the shining white of the transfigured Christ, the white robes of the redeemed, the white horse of the conqueror and the great white throne of judgment.

== Colours: scarlet and red
kind: symbol
forms: scarlet, red, crimson, red as crimson
refs: Josh 2:18,21; Isa 1:18; Isa 63:2; Nah 2:3; Rev 6:4; Rev 12:3; Rev 17:3-4; Rev 18:12,16
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The colour of blood: sin ("though your sins be as scarlet", Isa 1:18), war (the red horse of Rev 6), and the dragon and the harlot. Rahab's scarlet cord, which saved her house, has often been seen as a picture of salvation through blood.

== Colours: purple
kind: symbol
forms: purple
refs: Judg 8:26; Esth 8:15; Prov 31:22; Mark 15:17,20; Luke 16:19; John 19:2,5; Acts 16:14; Rev 17:4; Rev 18:12,16
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Purple dye was rare and costly, so purple meant royalty and wealth. The soldiers put a purple robe on Jesus to mock him as king; the rich man of the parable "was clothed in purple and fine linen".

== Colours: black and pale
kind: symbol
forms: black, blackness, pale
refs: Zech 6:2,6; Rev 6:5-8,12; Jude 1:13
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
In Revelation's four horses, black stands for famine and the pale (greenish-grey, the colour of a corpse) horse for death. "Blackness of darkness" is the doom of false teachers (Jude 13).

== Precious stones
kind: symbol
forms: jasper, sapphire, chalcedony, emerald, sardonyx, sardius, sardine, carnelian, chrysolite, chrysolyte, beryl, topaz, chrysoprase, chrysoprasus, jacinth, amethyst, agate, onyx, pearl, pearls, precious stones, gold
refs: Exod 28:17-21; Exod 39:10-14; Isa 54:11-12; Ezek 28:13; Rev 4:3; Rev 21:11,18-21
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The beauty, worth and glory of God and of the new Jerusalem, whose walls and foundations are of precious stones, its gates of pearl and its street of pure gold. The twelve stones echo those on the high priest's breastpiece, bearing the names of God's people.
