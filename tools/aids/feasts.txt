# Feasts and holy days

== The Sabbath
kind: feast
forms: sabbath, sabbaths, sabbath day
anywhere: yes
refs: Gen 2:2-3; Exod 20:8-11; Exod 31:13-17; Deut 5:12-15; Isa 58:13-14; Mark 2:27-28; Luke 4:16; Heb 4:9-10
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
The seventh day of the week (from Friday sunset to Saturday sunset), kept holy as a day of rest and worship. God rested on the seventh day of creation, and the Sabbath was a sign of his covenant with Israel. No work was done; in Jesus' day people met in the synagogue to hear the Scriptures read. Jesus called himself Lord of the Sabbath, and Christians have kept the first day, the day of his resurrection, as the Lord's Day.

== Passover
kind: feast
forms: passover, passovers
anywhere: yes
refs: Exod 12:1-28; Lev 23:5; Num 9:1-14; Deut 16:1-8; Matt 26:17-30; Luke 22:7-20; John 1:29; 1Cor 5:7
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The spring feast remembering the night God freed Israel from Egypt: the angel of death "passed over" the houses marked with a lamb's blood. Each family ate a roast lamb with unleavened bread and bitter herbs, on the evening of the 14th of Nisan (March or April). Jesus was crucified at Passover; the New Testament calls him "Christ our Passover" (1 Cor 5:7), and the Lord's Supper began at a Passover meal.

== The Feast of Unleavened Bread
kind: feast
forms: feast of unleavened bread, festival of unleavened bread, days of unleavened bread, unleavened bread
anywhere: no
refs: Exod 12:15-20; Exod 12:39; Exod 13:3-10; Exod 23:15; Lev 23:6-8; Num 28:17; Deut 16:3-8; 2Chr 30:13,21; 2Chr 35:17; Ezra 6:22; Matt 26:17; Mark 14:1,12; Luke 22:1,7; Acts 12:3; Acts 20:6; 1Cor 5:8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
Seven days straight after Passover when no bread with yeast (leaven) was eaten, and all leaven was cleared from the house. It recalled the haste of leaving Egypt, with no time for dough to rise. Paul uses it as a picture of a life cleared of malice and wickedness (1 Cor 5:7-8).

== Firstfruits
kind: feast
forms: firstfruits, firstfruit, first fruits, first-fruits, first of the firstfruits
anywhere: yes
refs: Exod 23:16,19; Lev 23:9-14; Num 18:12-13; Deut 26:1-11; Prov 3:9; Rom 8:23; 1Cor 15:20-23; Jas 1:18; Rev 14:4
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The first and best of the harvest, given to God before the rest was used, as thanks that all of it came from him. A sheaf of the first barley was waved before the Lord the day after the Sabbath of Passover week. Paul calls the risen Christ "the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Cor 15:20): the first of the great harvest of the resurrection.

== Pentecost (the Feast of Weeks)
kind: feast
forms: pentecost, feast of weeks, festival of weeks, feast of harvest, festival of harvest, harvest festival
anywhere: yes
refs: Exod 23:16; Exod 34:22; Lev 23:15-21; Num 28:26-31; Deut 16:9-12; Acts 2:1-41; Acts 20:16; 1Cor 16:8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The harvest feast held fifty days (seven weeks) after Passover, when two loaves from the new wheat were offered. "Pentecost" is Greek for "fiftieth". Jews from many lands came to Jerusalem for it, which is why so many nations heard the apostles when the Holy Spirit was poured out at Pentecost (Acts 2).

== The Feast of Trumpets
kind: feast
forms: feast of trumpets, festival of trumpets, blowing of trumpets, blowing of the trumpets, day of blowing, day for you to blow the trumpets, trumpet blasts
anywhere: no
refs: Lev 23:23-25; Num 29:1-6
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
A holy day of rest on the first day of the seventh month (September or October), announced by the blowing of trumpets (the ram's horn). It began the most solemn month of Israel's year, leading to the Day of Atonement and the Feast of Tabernacles. In later Judaism it became the New Year (Rosh Hashanah).

== The Day of Atonement
kind: feast
forms: day of atonement, day of the atonement, day of atonements, atonement day
anywhere: yes
refs: Lev 16; Lev 23:26-32; Num 29:7-11; Heb 9:6-14; Heb 9:24-28; Heb 10:1-14
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The holiest day of Israel's year (Yom Kippur), on the tenth day of the seventh month: a day of fasting and confession. Only on this day did the high priest enter the Most Holy Place, with the blood of a sacrifice, to make atonement for the sins of the whole nation; a second goat, the scapegoat, carried their sins away into the wilderness. Hebrews shows Jesus as the great High Priest who entered heaven itself with his own blood, once for all.

== The Feast of Tabernacles (Booths)
kind: feast
forms: feast of tabernacles, feast of the tabernacles, feast of booths, festival of booths, festival of shelters, feast of shelters, feast of ingathering, festival of ingathering
anywhere: yes
refs: Exod 23:16; Lev 23:33-43; Num 29:12-38; Deut 16:13-15; Deut 31:10-13; Neh 8:13-18; Zech 14:16-19; John 7:2,37-39
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The joyful autumn feast at the end of harvest, from the 15th of the seventh month for seven days. Families lived in shelters (booths) made of branches, to remember Israel's tents in the wilderness. Water was poured out at the temple each day and great lamps were lit; on the last day Jesus stood and offered "living water" (John 7:37-38).

== The New Moon
kind: feast
forms: new moon, new moons, new moon's, new moon festival, new moon festivals, new moon feast, new moon feasts, new moon celebrations, beginnings of your months, beginning of your months
anywhere: yes
refs: Num 10:10; Num 28:11-15; 1Sam 20:5,18,24; 2Kgs 4:23; Ps 81:3; Isa 1:13-14; Isa 66:23; Amos 8:5; Col 2:16
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The first day of each month in Israel's calendar, which followed the moon. Trumpets were blown, extra offerings made and work often stopped, with family meals (1 Sam 20). Paul says such days were "a shadow of things to come", while the reality is Christ (Col 2:16-17).

== The appointed feasts
kind: feast
forms: appointed feasts, appointed feast, appointed festivals, appointed festival, solemn feasts, solemn feast, set feasts, appointed times, feasts of the lord, festivals of the lord, appointed seasons
anywhere: yes
refs: Exod 23:14-17; Lev 23; Num 28-29; Deut 16:16-17; Ezek 46:9-11; Hos 2:11
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
The holy days God gave Israel (Lev 23): the weekly Sabbath, and the yearly feasts of Passover and Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, Weeks (Pentecost), Trumpets, the Day of Atonement and Tabernacles. Three times a year, at Unleavened Bread, Weeks and Tabernacles, every man was to appear before the Lord at his sanctuary. They marked Israel's year with God's saving acts and the harvests he gave.

== The Sabbath year
kind: feast
forms: seventh year, year of release, release, sabbath of rest, year of rest, rest for the land, the land shall keep a sabbath, cancel debts, cancellation of debts, cancel the debts
anywhere: no
refs: Exod 23:10-11; Lev 25:1-7,18-22; Deut 15:1-11; Deut 31:10-13; Neh 10:31; Jer 34:14; 2Chr 36:21
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Every seventh year the fields were left unsown so the land could rest, and what grew by itself was left for the poor and the animals. Debts between Israelites were cancelled, and Hebrew servants were set free. It taught that the land was the Lord's; the exile's seventy years were linked to the Sabbath years Israel had not kept (2 Chr 36:21).

== The Jubilee
kind: feast
forms: jubilee, jubile, year of jubilee, year of jubile, the jubilee year
anywhere: yes
refs: Lev 25:8-55; Lev 27:17-24; Num 36:4; Isa 61:1-2; Luke 4:16-21
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Every fiftieth year, after seven Sabbath years, a trumpet on the Day of Atonement proclaimed liberty through the land. Land that had been sold went back to the family it was first given to, and Israelites who had sold themselves as servants went free. Jesus read Isaiah's "acceptable year of the Lord", which echoes the Jubilee, as fulfilled in himself (Luke 4:18-21).

== Purim
kind: feast
forms: purim, pur
anywhere: yes
refs: Esth 3:7; Esth 9:20-32
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A joyful feast on the 14th and 15th of the month Adar (February or March), remembering how God saved the Jews in Persia from Haman's plot, as told in Esther. "Pur" means "lot": Haman cast lots to choose the day to destroy them. It was kept with feasting, gifts to one another and gifts to the poor.

== The Feast of Dedication (Hanukkah)
kind: feast
forms: feast of the dedication, feast of dedication, festival of dedication, festival of the dedication, hanukkah
anywhere: yes
refs: John 10:22-23
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
A winter feast of eight days (in December), remembering the cleansing and rededication of the temple in 164 BC, after the Maccabees freed it from the Greek king Antiochus, who had defiled it. It was also called the Feast of Lights, as lamps were lit in every home. Jesus was in the temple at this feast when he said, "I and my Father are one" (John 10:30).

== Fasting
kind: feast
forms: fast, fasted, fasting, fastings, fasts, fastest, the fast
anywhere: no
refs: Lev 16:29-31; Judg 20:26; 1Sam 7:6; 2Sam 12:16-23; Ezra 8:21-23; Neh 9:1; Esth 4:3,16; Ps 35:13; Isa 58:3-7; Jer 36:9; Dan 9:3; Joel 2:12-15; Jonah 3:5; Zech 7:3-5; Zech 8:19; Matt 4:2; Matt 6:16-18; Matt 9:14-15; Mark 2:18-20; Luke 2:37; Luke 18:12; Acts 13:2-3; Acts 14:23; Acts 27:9
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
Going without food for a time, to humble oneself before God: in sorrow for sin, in grief, or when seeking God's help. The Law commanded only one fast, on the Day of Atonement ("the fast" of Acts 27:9); others were added for times of trouble. Pharisees fasted twice a week; Jesus taught that fasting should be done for God's eyes, not to be seen.

== The preparation day
kind: feast
forms: preparation, day of preparation, preparation day, day before the sabbath
anywhere: no
refs: Matt 27:62; Mark 15:42; Luke 23:54; John 19:14,31,42
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The day before the Sabbath (Friday), when food was cooked and everything readied so no work need be done on the Sabbath. Jesus died on a preparation day; his body had to be buried before sunset, when the Sabbath began.

== A Sabbath day's journey
kind: feast
forms: sabbath day's journey, sabbath day journey, sabbath day's walk, sabbath day walk
anywhere: yes
refs: Acts 1:12
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The distance the rabbis allowed a person to walk on the Sabbath: about 2,000 cubits, roughly two-thirds of a mile (1 km). Acts uses it to say how near the Mount of Olives was to Jerusalem.

== The hours of the day
kind: feast
forms: third hour, sixth hour, ninth hour, seventh hour, tenth hour, eleventh hour, first hour, hour of prayer
anywhere: yes
refs: Matt 20:1-12; Matt 27:45-46; Mark 15:25,33-34; John 1:39; John 4:6,52; John 19:14; Acts 2:15; Acts 3:1; Acts 10:3,9,30
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, The Temple: Its Ministry and Services
In Jesus' day the daylight was counted in twelve hours from sunrise (about 6 a.m.). The third hour was about 9 a.m., the sixth about noon and the ninth about 3 p.m.; the morning and evening sacrifices at the temple were the usual hours of prayer. John's Gospel may count from midnight, as the Romans did.

== The watches of the night
kind: feast
forms: watch, watches, morning watch, middle watch, fourth watch, second watch, third watch
anywhere: no
refs: Exod 14:24; Judg 7:19; 1Sam 11:11; Ps 63:6; Ps 90:4; Ps 119:148; Lam 2:19; Matt 14:25; Mark 6:48; Mark 13:35; Luke 12:38
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The night was divided into watches, the times when guards took turns. The Old Testament counts three watches; in Roman times there were four, of three hours each, from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. Jesus walked on the water "in the fourth watch", just before dawn.

== The Shema
kind: feast
forms: hear o israel, listen o israel, hear israel, listen israel
anywhere: yes
refs: Deut 6:4-9; Deut 11:13-21; Num 15:37-41; Mark 12:29-30
sources: Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
"Hear, O Israel: the Lord our God is one Lord" (Deut 6:4), the confession of faith every Jew said morning and evening, named from its first word in Hebrew, "Shema" ("Hear"). Jesus quoted it as the first and greatest commandment, with the call to love God with all your heart, soul, mind and strength.
