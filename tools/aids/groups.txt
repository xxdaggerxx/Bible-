# Groups and offices in Jesus' day

== Pharisees
kind: group
forms: pharisee, pharisees, pharisee's, pharisees'
anywhere: yes
refs: Matt 5:20; Matt 23:1-36; Mark 7:1-13; Luke 7:36-50; Luke 18:9-14; John 3:1; Acts 23:6-9; Phil 3:5
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
The strictest religious party among the Jews, known for careful keeping of the Law and of the "tradition of the elders" added to it, especially on the Sabbath, tithing and ritual washing. They believed in the resurrection and in angels. Many opposed Jesus, who rebuked their hypocrisy, but some, like Nicodemus and Paul, came to believe.

== Sadducees
kind: group
forms: sadducee, sadducees
anywhere: yes
refs: Matt 3:7; Matt 16:1-12; Matt 22:23-34; Mark 12:18-27; Acts 4:1-2; Acts 5:17; Acts 23:6-8
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
A party of wealthy, priestly families who controlled the temple and much of the council. They accepted only the written Law and denied the resurrection, angels and spirits. They worked with Rome to keep their power, and led the opposition to the apostles' preaching of Jesus' resurrection.

== Scribes (teachers of the law)
kind: group
forms: scribe, scribes, teacher of the law, teachers of the law, teachers of religious law, teacher of religious law, experts in the law, expert in the law, lawyer, lawyers, doctors of the law, doctor of the law, teachers of the scriptures
anywhere: yes
not: 2Sam 8:17; 2Sam 20:25; 2Kgs 12:10; 2Kgs 18:18,37; 2Kgs 19:2; 2Kgs 22:3-12; Jer 36:10-32; Esth 3:12; Esth 8:9; Acts 19:35; Titus 3:13
refs: Ezra 7:6,10-12; Neh 8:1-9; Matt 7:29; Matt 23:2-36; Mark 12:28-40; Luke 10:25; Luke 11:45-52
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
Experts who copied, studied and taught the Law of Moses and how it applied to daily life. Ezra was the great scribe after the exile. In Jesus' day most scribes were Pharisees and sat on the council; people were amazed that Jesus taught "as one having authority, and not as the scribes" (Matt 7:29). In the early kingdom a "scribe" was a royal secretary.

== The council (Sanhedrin)
kind: group
forms: sanhedrin, council, high council, supreme council, jewish council, ruling council, councils
anywhere: no
refs: Matt 5:22; Matt 26:59; Mark 14:55; Mark 15:1; Luke 22:66; John 11:47; Acts 4:15; Acts 5:21,27,34,41; Acts 6:12,15; Acts 22:30; Acts 23:1,6,15,20,28; Acts 24:20
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
The highest Jewish court in Jerusalem, of 71 members: chief priests, elders and scribes, led by the high priest. Under Rome it governed religious matters but could not carry out a death sentence, which is why Jesus was handed to Pilate. Peter, John, Stephen and Paul were all tried before it.

== Herodians
kind: group
forms: herodians
anywhere: yes
refs: Matt 22:16; Mark 3:6; Mark 12:13
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Jews who supported the rule of Herod's family, and so Rome. Though usually opposed to the Pharisees, they joined them in plotting against Jesus and in trying to trap him with the question of paying taxes to Caesar.

== Zealots
kind: group
forms: zealot, zelotes, the zealot, canaanite, cananaean, cananite
anywhere: no
refs: Matt 10:4; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Jews zealous to free their nation from Rome, some by violence. One of the twelve apostles, Simon, was called the Zealot ("Canaanite" in the King James Version comes from the Aramaic word for "zealous", not the land of Canaan).

== Samaritans
kind: group
forms: samaritan, samaritans
anywhere: yes
refs: 2Kgs 17:24-41; Ezra 4:1-5; Luke 9:52-56; Luke 10:30-37; Luke 17:16; John 4:4-42; John 8:48; Acts 8:5-25
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The people of Samaria, between Judea and Galilee, descended from Israelites left after the Assyrian conquest and foreigners settled there. They worshipped on Mount Gerizim and accepted only the five books of Moses. Jews and Samaritans despised each other, which is why Jesus' talk with the Samaritan woman and his parable of the Good Samaritan were so startling.

== Tax collectors
kind: group
forms: publican, publicans, tax collector, tax collectors, tax-collector, tax-collectors, chief tax collector, chief among the publicans, collector of taxes
anywhere: yes
refs: Matt 5:46; Matt 9:9-13; Matt 11:19; Matt 21:31-32; Luke 3:12-13; Luke 15:1-2; Luke 18:9-14; Luke 19:1-10
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
Jews who collected taxes for Rome, often taking more than was owed and keeping the rest. They were hated as traitors and cheats, and ranked with "sinners". Jesus ate with them and called one, Matthew, to be an apostle; Zacchaeus, a chief tax collector, repaid four times what he had taken.

== Gentiles
kind: group
forms: gentile, gentiles, heathen, the nations, uncircumcised
anywhere: no
refs: Gen 12:3; Isa 42:6; Isa 49:6; Matt 4:15; Matt 28:19; Luke 2:32; Acts 10:45; Acts 13:46-48; Acts 15:7-19; Rom 1:16; Rom 11:11-25; Gal 3:8,14; Eph 2:11-22; Eph 3:6
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
All peoples who were not Jews. Jews kept apart from them, would not eat with them, and kept them out of the inner temple courts. Yet God promised Abraham that all nations would be blessed through him; the New Testament tells how the gospel went to the Gentiles and made Jew and Gentile one in Christ.

== Proselytes
kind: group
forms: proselyte, proselytes, converts to judaism, convert to judaism, converts, proselyte to judaism
anywhere: no
refs: Matt 23:15; Acts 2:10; Acts 6:5; Acts 13:43
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Gentiles who became Jews, accepting circumcision and the whole Law. Many others, called "God-fearers" or "devout" (like Cornelius), worshipped Israel's God without fully converting; many of the first Gentile believers came from among them.

== Rabbis
kind: group
forms: rabbi, rabbis, rabboni, master, teacher
anywhere: no
refs: Matt 23:7-8; John 1:38,49; John 3:2; John 20:16; Mark 9:5; Mark 10:51
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
"Rabbi" means "my master" or "my teacher": a respectful title for a teacher of the Law, who gathered disciples to learn from him and live with him. Jesus was called Rabbi; Mary Magdalene called him "Rabboni" at the tomb.

== Disciples
kind: group
forms: disciple, disciples, disciple's
anywhere: no
refs: Matt 5:1; Matt 10:1; Matt 28:19; Mark 2:18; Luke 14:26-33; John 1:35-37; John 8:31; John 13:35; Acts 11:26
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary; Edersheim, Sketches of Jewish Social Life
A disciple was a learner who followed a teacher, lived with him and copied his way of life. John the Baptist and the Pharisees had disciples; Jesus called many to follow him and chose twelve as apostles. Jesus told his followers to make disciples of all nations.

== Patriarchs
kind: group
forms: patriarch, patriarchs, fathers
anywhere: no
refs: Acts 2:29; Acts 7:8-9; Heb 7:4; Rom 9:5
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
The founding fathers of Israel: Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and Jacob's twelve sons, the heads of the tribes. The New Testament also calls David and Abraham patriarchs.

== The twelve tribes
kind: group
forms: twelve tribes, tribes of israel, twelve tribes of israel, tribe, tribes
anywhere: no
refs: Gen 49:1-28; Num 1:1-46; Num 2; Josh 13-21; Matt 19:28; Luke 22:30; Acts 26:7; Jas 1:1; Rev 7:4-8; Rev 21:12
sources: Easton's Bible Dictionary
Israel was made up of twelve tribes, each descended from one of Jacob's sons (with Joseph's two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh, counted as tribes and Levi given no land). Each tribe received its own share of the land. Jesus promised the apostles they would sit on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes.
